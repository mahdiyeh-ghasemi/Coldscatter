# Citation, copyright, and third-party material

## Project authorship and copyright

Coldscatter is developed and maintained by Mahdiyeh Ghasemi.

Copyright in the original Coldscatter source code and documentation is
identified in the repository's `LICENSE` file. The project is distributed
under the MIT License. Preserve the copyright notice and the complete MIT
permission notice in copies or substantial portions of the software, as
required by that license.

The MIT License does not itself impose a requirement to cite Coldscatter
in an academic paper. The project maintainer nevertheless requests a
scholarly citation whenever the software materially contributes to
published research. This academic request is separate from the legal
conditions of the MIT License.

## How to cite Coldscatter

The repository includes `CITATION.cff`, which can be used by GitHub and
citation managers. Cite the exact version used. Once a versioned release
has been archived and assigned a DOI, prefer the DOI-based citation for
that release.

Until an archival DOI is available, a provisional citation is:

> Ghasemi, M. (2026). *Coldscatter* (version 0.1.1) [Computer software].
> GitHub. https://github.com/mahdiyeh-ghasemi/Coldscatter

Replace the version and year with those of the version actually used.
Do not invent or imply a DOI before one has been issued.

## What to report in a research paper

In addition to citing the software, report enough information to reproduce
the calculation, including:

- Coldscatter version or commit identifier;
- collision energy, units, reduced mass, and channel thresholds;
- channel basis, partial waves, and entrance channel;
- potential model and all relevant parameters;
- long-range reference coefficients and matching radii, if used;
- radial grid and convergence checks;
- any modifications to the software or local scripts.

Cite the original publications for physical models, constants, numerical
methods, and experimental data independently of the software citation.
Citing Coldscatter does not replace citing the underlying scientific work.

## Third-party software, code, data, and figures

Coldscatter depends on NumPy and SciPy; Matplotlib is an optional
dependency used by plotting and example functionality, and pytest is
used for tests. Their names and licenses remain those of their respective
authors and projects. Consult the license and notice files distributed
with the exact versions and binary packages used.

Do not copy third-party code, figures, tables, or datasets into this
repository without checking the applicable license or permission terms.
Where reuse is permitted, retain required copyright and license notices
and provide scholarly attribution where appropriate. A bibliographic
citation alone does not replace a license notice when the license
requires one.

The license obligations of a third-party component depend on the
component and the form in which it is redistributed. A license inventory
from one local Python environment is not a universal license manifest
for every Coldscatter installation or binary distribution.

## Publisher and source attribution

Coldscatter is currently distributed from its GitHub repository. It has
not been assigned a journal publisher or archival DOI in this development
version. Do not attribute the software to a scientific publisher or claim
that it has been peer reviewed unless that status changes and can be
verified.

When referring to an external article, dataset, figure, or codebase,
identify its actual authors, title, publication venue or publisher,
year, and persistent identifier where available. Follow the source's
license and attribution requirements.

## Research authorship

Use of Coldscatter does not automatically make its maintainer an author
of a paper. Authorship should follow the relevant journal and research
community's contribution criteria. A software citation and
acknowledgement are appropriate ways to credit software use when
authorship criteria are not met.

## Scope

This document describes the project's attribution policy and is not
formal legal advice. Copyright exceptions, license interpretation, and
redistribution obligations can depend on jurisdiction and circumstances.
